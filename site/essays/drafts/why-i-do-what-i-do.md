I don't want to die. I would very much like to live forever. At least, and at the moment, I would like to live a lot longer than the +40 years I expect to have left, and in better health than I can expect towards the end. One avenue I see towards reaching that goal is understanding how human consciousness works. This might allow us to (re)create, fix, preserve, and possibly transfer people's consciousnesses. At the moment, it does not seem that OpenAI, Anthropic, or other AI labs are brute-forcing their way to AGI. And if they do succeed, it's not at all clear that this intelligence will resemble ours, inform us how our consciousness works, and/or offer us what is needed for me to survive, and others, of course.

This is a path that I currently think has the best chance of success. This path leads towards the discovery of a scientific theory that explains how consciousness works, how it can emerge from previously unconscious organisms, and how it was (or plausibly could be) developed by a mindless process like natural selection. Unfortunately, I don't have the background, knowledge, and skills in the subject matter. And I am by myself, alone, with very limited resources. So, I do not see myself succeeding where countless others before me have failed.

However, there is something I can contribute to the endeavor. It might be small, though it might prove significant. Until recently, theory development (in the social sciences) was a subpar enterprise. Developing a theory was done on the side, and getting _just_ a theory, without (new) data, published was a near-futile endeavor. There was, and still is, little to no training in theory development skills, and theory development methods are either non-existent or unknown to potential users. This is where I make my small contribution: developing methods, teaching theory development skills, promoting change in research output, and pushing the recognition of theoretical work.

I like the word "fulcrum" (ever since I learned of it in the television series The Blacklist). For the unaware and interested reader, here is the [Wikipedia definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulcrum) (Confession: I had to look it up when I saw The Blacklist). As Archimedes might have said, "Give me a lever long enough and a fulcrum on which to place it, and I shall move the world." I hope that my academic work can be (part of) that fulcrum that will allow others to leverage their scientific endeavors: make light(er) work of the heavy task of figuring out how we tick.
